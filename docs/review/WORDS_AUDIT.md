### Reading Between the Lines (`reading-between-the-lines`) — 8 questions

1. *feel* — (1) It was a quiet evening at home. (2) Then thunder rumbled across the sky. (3) Bonnie dived under the kitchen table and hid her face in her paws. (4) Sarah sat down on the floor and stroked her gently.
   - **Q:** How did Bonnie feel when the thunder rumbled? **Answer:** frightened · *not:* sleepy / excited
   - **Evidence:** (3) “Bonnie dived under the kitchen table and hid her face in her paws.” — *Which sentence shows how Bonnie felt?*
   - **Bonnie:** Bonnie dived under the table and hid her face when the thunder rumbled. That suggests she was frightened.
2. *feel* — (1) Leo opened his birthday present. (2) Inside was a shiny red bike. (3) “Wow!” shouted Leo, jumping up and down. (4) “Can I ride it now?”
   - **Q:** How did Leo feel about his present? **Answer:** very happy · *not:* upset / bored
   - **Evidence:** (3) ““Wow!” shouted Leo, jumping up and down.” or (4) ““Can I ride it now?”” — *Which sentence shows how Leo felt?*
   - **Bonnie:** Leo shouted “Wow!”, jumped up and down and wanted to ride it straight away. That shows he was very happy with his bike.
3. *feel* — (1) Ben looked out of the window and sighed. (2) Rain was pouring down on the garden. (3) His football sat by the door, still in its bag. (4) “We can't play in the park today,” said Dad.
   - **Q:** How did Ben feel? **Answer:** disappointed · *not:* proud / scared
   - **Evidence:** (1) “Ben looked out of the window and sighed.” — *Which sentence shows how Ben felt?*
   - **Bonnie:** Ben sighed as he looked at the rain. People often sigh when they are disappointed, and he could not play football in the park.
4. *feel* — (1) Oliver's grandad was coming to stay for the weekend. (2) He was due to arrive at four o'clock. (3) All afternoon, Oliver kept running to the window to look for his car. (4) When the doorbell rang, Oliver raced to open it.
   - **Q:** How did Oliver feel about Grandad's visit? **Answer:** excited · *not:* worried / cross
   - **Evidence:** (3) “All afternoon, Oliver kept running to the window to look for his car.” or (4) “When the doorbell rang, Oliver raced to open it.” — *Which sentence shows how Oliver felt?*
   - **Bonnie:** Oliver kept running to the window and raced to open the door. That shows he was excited to see Grandad.
5. *cause* — (1) Mia's tooth had been wobbly for a week. (2) At lunch, she bit into a crunchy apple. (3) Mia gasped and held out her hand. (4) There, in her palm, was a tiny white tooth!
   - **Q:** What had happened to Mia? **Answer:** Her wobbly tooth came out · *not:* She dropped her apple / She hurt her hand
   - **Evidence:** (4) “There, in her palm, was a tiny white tooth!” — *Which sentence shows you?*
   - **Bonnie:** There was a tiny white tooth in Mia's hand, and her tooth had been wobbly. So her wobbly tooth had come out!
6. *cause* — (1) Priya carried the big birthday cake very slowly. (2) She looked down at her feet with every step. (3) She held the plate tightly with both hands. (4) At last, she put it on the table and smiled.
   - **Q:** Why did Priya walk so slowly? **Answer:** She did not want to drop the cake · *not:* She was very tired / She was lost
   - **Evidence:** (2) “She looked down at her feet with every step.” or (3) “She held the plate tightly with both hands.” — *Which sentence shows you?*
   - **Bonnie:** Priya watched every step and held the plate tightly with both hands. She was being careful not to drop the cake.
7. *cause* — (1) The squirrel sat on the fence, nibbling a nut. (2) A big dog came bounding across the grass, barking loudly. (3) The squirrel dropped its nut and shot up the tree. (4) It stayed on the highest branch for a long time.
   - **Q:** Why did the squirrel run up the tree? **Answer:** It was scared of the dog · *not:* It wanted more nuts / It was playing a game
   - **Evidence:** (2) “A big dog came bounding across the grass, barking loudly.” or (3) “The squirrel dropped its nut and shot up the tree.” — *Which sentence shows you?*
   - **Bonnie:** The squirrel dropped its nut and shot up the tree when a barking dog came bounding over. It was scared of the dog.
8. *cause* — (1) The little seed sat in the ground. (2) It did not move. It made no sound. (3) Then rain came down and the sun came out, (4) and soon a green shoot began to sprout!
   - **Q:** What helped the seed to grow? **Answer:** the rain and the sun · *not:* the wind and the snow / a hungry bird
   - **Evidence:** (3) “Then rain came down and the sun came out,” — *Which line of the poem tells you?*
   - **Bonnie:** The poem says rain came down and the sun came out, and then the shoot began to sprout. Seeds need water and warmth to grow.

### What Happens Next? (`what-happens-next`) — 8 questions

1. *predict* — (1) Jack built a tall tower of blocks on the floor. (2) It was even taller than his teddy bear. (3) His baby sister crawled over and reached up to grab the top block. (4) Jack shouted, “Wait!”
   - **Q:** What is the best guess for what happens next? **Answer:** The tower falls down · *not:* The baby falls asleep / Jack goes outside to play
   - **Evidence:** (3) “His baby sister crawled over and reached up to grab the top block.” — *Which sentence…*
   - **Bonnie:** Jack's baby sister reached up to grab the top block of a very tall tower. So the best guess is that the tower falls down.
2. *predict* — (1) Mia and Gran were having a picnic in the park. (2) They ate sandwiches and strawberries. (3) Then big grey clouds rolled across the sky, and Mia felt a cold drop on her nose. (4) Gran reached for her umbrella.
   - **Q:** What is the best guess for what happens next? **Answer:** It rains and Gran opens her umbrella · *not:* The sun comes out and it gets very hot / Mia goes for a swim in the pond
   - **Evidence:** (3) “Then big grey clouds rolled across the sky, and Mia felt a cold drop on her nose.” or (4) “Gran reached for her umbrella.” — *Which sentence…*
   - **Bonnie:** Grey clouds came, Mia felt a drop and Gran reached for her umbrella. So the best guess is that it rains.
3. *predict* — (1) Tom and Dad were having lunch on the beach. (2) Tom put his sandwich down on the towel and ran to the sea. (3) A hungry seagull landed right next to the sandwich. (4) It looked at the sandwich, then at Tom, far away in the water.
   - **Q:** What is the best guess for what happens next? **Answer:** The seagull grabs the sandwich · *not:* Dad eats the sandwich / The seagull flies away scared
   - **Evidence:** (3) “A hungry seagull landed right next to the sandwich.” or (4) “It looked at the sandwich, then at Tom, far away in the water.” — *Which sentence…*
   - **Bonnie:** A hungry seagull landed next to the sandwich while Tom was far away in the sea. So the best guess is that the seagull grabs it!
4. *predict* — (1) “Don't forget your lunchbox!” called Mum. (2) But Ava was in a hurry and ran out of the door. (3) Her lunchbox stayed on the kitchen table. (4) At lunchtime, Ava opened her school bag.
   - **Q:** What is the best guess for what happens next? **Answer:** Ava finds her lunchbox is not there · *not:* Ava finds her lunchbox in her bag / Ava goes to the shop
   - **Evidence:** (2) “But Ava was in a hurry and ran out of the door.” or (3) “Her lunchbox stayed on the kitchen table.” — *Which sentence…*
   - **Bonnie:** Ava ran out in a hurry and her lunchbox stayed on the kitchen table. So when she opens her bag, the best guess is that her lunchbox will not be there.
5. *predict* — (1) The hen sat on her nest for many days. (2) One morning, she heard a tiny tap, tap, tap. (3) A small crack appeared in one of her eggs. (4) The hen moved to one side and watched.
   - **Q:** What is the best guess for what happens next? **Answer:** A chick hatches out of the egg · *not:* The hen lays a new egg / The egg rolls into the pond
   - **Evidence:** (2) “One morning, she heard a tiny tap, tap, tap.” or (3) “A small crack appeared in one of her eggs.” — *Which sentence…*
   - **Bonnie:** There was a tapping sound and then a crack in the egg. So the best guess is that a chick is about to hatch!
6. *predict* — (1) It was a hot, sunny day at the park. (2) Priya bought a strawberry ice lolly. (3) She was so busy talking to her friend that she forgot to eat it. (4) The lolly got softer and softer, and began to slide off its stick.
   - **Q:** What is the best guess for what happens next? **Answer:** The lolly falls off the stick · *not:* The lolly freezes again / Priya buys a sun hat
   - **Evidence:** (3) “She was so busy talking to her friend that she forgot to eat it.” or (4) “The lolly got softer and softer, and began to slide off its stick.” — *Which sentence…*
   - **Bonnie:** The lolly was getting softer and sliding off its stick, and Priya was not watching it. So the best guess is that it falls off!
7. *predict* — (1) It was a sunny Saturday morning. (2) Bonnie was snoozing in her basket. (3) Then Sarah picked up Bonnie's lead and put on her wellies. (4) Bonnie jumped up and ran to the front door, wagging her tail.
   - **Q:** What is the best guess for what happens next? **Answer:** Sarah takes Bonnie for a walk · *not:* Sarah has a bath / Bonnie goes back to sleep
   - **Evidence:** (3) “Then Sarah picked up Bonnie's lead and put on her wellies.” or (4) “Bonnie jumped up and ran to the front door, wagging her tail.” — *Which sentence…*
   - **Bonnie:** Sarah picked up the lead and put on her wellies, and Bonnie ran to the door. So the best guess is that they go for a walk.
8. *predict* — (1) Ben and his dad climbed to the top of the hill. (2) Ben's red kite flew high in the sky. (3) Suddenly, a strong gust of wind tugged the string right out of Ben's hand. (4) “Oh no!” cried Ben.
   - **Q:** What is the best guess for what happens next? **Answer:** The kite blows away · *not:* The kite lands in Ben's hands / Ben flies the kite even higher
   - **Evidence:** (3) “Suddenly, a strong gust of wind tugged the string right out of Ben's hand.” or (4) ““Oh no!” cried Ben.” — *Which sentence…*
   - **Bonnie:** The wind tugged the string out of Ben's hand, and he cried “Oh no!”. With nobody holding it, the best guess is that the kite blows away.

### Story Detective (`story-detective`) — 9 questions

1. *order* — (1) Ava wanted to make a birthday card for Gran. (2) First, she folded a piece of paper in half. (3) Next, she drew a big yellow flower on the front. (4) Then she wrote “Happy Birthday” inside. (5) Finally, she put the card in an envelope.
   - **Order:** 1. Ava folded the paper. 2. Ava drew a flower. 3. Ava wrote inside the card. 4. Ava put the card in an envelope.
   - **Bonnie:** First Ava folded the paper, next she drew a flower, then she wrote inside, and finally she put the card in an envelope.
2. *order* — (1) Leo left the garden gate open. (2) Bonnie spotted a rabbit and ran out into the lane. (3) Leo and Dad searched everywhere for her. (4) At last they found Bonnie in a field, sniffing a rabbit hole.
   - **Order:** 1. The gate was left open. 2. Bonnie ran out after a rabbit. 3. Leo and Dad looked for Bonnie. 4. They found Bonnie in the field.
   - **Bonnie:** The gate was left open, so Bonnie could run out after a rabbit. Then Leo and Dad looked for her, and at last they found her in the field.
3. *order* — (1) Mia's class went on a trip to the farm. (2) Farmer Jones showed them the cows in the barn. (3) After that, they fed the lambs with bottles of milk. (4) Before they went home, everyone had a picnic by the duck pond.
   - **Order:** 1. The children saw the cows. 2. The children fed the lambs. 3. The children had a picnic.
   - **Bonnie:** First the children saw the cows, after that they fed the lambs, and then they had a picnic before they went home.

   - **Evidence:** (2) “Then the sun came out and shone all afternoon.” — *Which sentence tells you why?*

5. *cause* — (1) Tom's tummy rumbled loudly in class. (2) He had been in such a rush that morning that he had not eaten any breakfast. (3) At break time, his teacher gave him an apple.

   - **Evidence:** (2) “He had been in such a rush that morning that he had not eaten any breakfast.” — *Which sentence tells you why?*
   - **Bonnie:** Tom was in a rush and did not eat any breakfast, so he was hungry. That is why his tummy rumbled.
6. *cause* — (1) Gran's plants were drooping. (2) It had not rained for weeks. (3) Priya filled the watering can and gave each plant a long drink. (4) By the next morning, the plants were standing up tall again.
   - **Q:** Why did the plants stand up tall again? **Answer:** Priya gave them water · *not:* It snowed in the night / Gran bought new plants
   - **Evidence:** (3) “Priya filled the watering can and gave each plant a long drink.” — *Which sentence tells you why?*
   - **Bonnie:** Priya gave each plant a long drink of water. By the next morning, they were standing up tall again.

   - **Bonnie:** The plants had no rain for weeks, and after a drink they were standing up tall again. So before that, drooping means they were flopping down.

- **a after w:** After w, the sound in hot is often spelt with the letter a, like want, watch and wander.
- **Adding an ending:** When we add an ending that starts with a vowel, like ing, ed, er, est, or the ending y, the root word can change. We drop an e at the end. We double one last consonant after a short vowel sound. We change a y to i after a consonant, but not before ing. Often, we just add it!
